The Karim's Legacy

ESTABLISHED 1913 • JAMA MASJID, DELHI

🏛️ THE ORIGIN

In the shadow of Delhi's majestic Jama Masjid, in the year 1913, Haji Karimuddin — a master chef whose ancestors had served in the royal Mughal kitchens of Emperor Jahangir — opened a modest restaurant. He carried with him the closely guarded secrets of the imperial dastarkhwan: the precise art of dum pukht, the alchemy of hand-ground masalas perfected over generations, and the patience of slow charcoal tandoor cooking.

🔥 THE ROYAL KITCHEN

The Mughal emperors were among history's greatest gastronomes. Emperor Jahangir himself was known to dine on 72 dishes at a single feast. The royal khansamas — court chefs — guarded their recipes with their lives, passing them down orally from father to son. Haji Karimuddin was among the last descendants of this illustrious lineage, and he chose to share these treasures with the world while preserving every secret.

⭐ THE CENTURY OF FLAVOUR

Over a century later, Karim's remains faithful to the original 1913 recipes. Our kebabs are still marinated for hours in hand-ground spice pastes. Our biryanis are still sealed under dough and cooked over slow charcoal. Our nihari still simmers overnight. Every dish that leaves our kitchen carries the soul of the Mughal empire and the dedication of four generations.

🍽️ ARRIVAL IN IMPHAL

Now, the legendary flavours of 1913 arrive in the heart of Manipur. Karim's Imphal brings you the same regal hospitality, the same hand-ground masalas, the same charcoal-fired perfection — adapted with love for the discerning palates of the Northeast. Every meal here is a journey through time, a communion with history, a celebration of culinary artistry that has survived and thrived for over a hundred years.
